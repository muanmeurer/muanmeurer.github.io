      <h2>What is it?</h2>
      <p>Bridges have an annoying habit of extending underwater, where ordinary inspection drones can’t follow. Kingfisher is our answer: an amphibious drone designed to fly to a bridge, land on the water, and dive to inspect submerged structures using onboard sensors. The point is to collect inspection data—not water samples.</p>
      <h2>How does it work?</h2>
      <p>Four propellers handle the aerial leg. A sealed central body houses the electronics and a piston-driven ballast system that takes in and expels water to change buoyancy. That water is ballast, not a sample. Once submerged, Kingfisher uses sensors to inspect the structure, with teleoperation and autonomy both considered in the mission design.</p>
      <p>I've been working through the mechanical packaging, buoyancy, sensor integration, and the delightful problem of making one machine behave in two very different environments.</p>
