      <h2>What's the point?</h2>
      <p>MoonRanger needs to survive the trip to the Moon before it can do anything interesting on the surface. During transit, it sits inside a semi-enclosed garage on the lander. That garage could protect the rover from the cold—or hold onto enough internally generated heat to cook its avionics.</p>
      <p>My job was to figure out which, and whether the answer changed when the rover powered up to check in or charge its batteries.</p>

      <h2>How did I model it?</h2>
      <p>I built a finite-difference model in Thermal Desktop, with radiation to deep space, solar exposure, heat flowing through the lander and hold-down hardware, and heat generated by the rover itself. Then I tried different charging and checkout schedules to see where the temperatures landed.</p>

  <h2>So, would the rover overheat?</h2>
  <p>It depends on how impatient we are. An aggressive one-hour-charge / three-hour-rest schedule pushed the avionics past their thermal limit. With a more realistic duty cycle, the garage was a pretty useful thermal buffer.</p>

  <h2>What did I take away?</h2>
  <p>The garage wasn't essential for cold survival—heaters could handle that—but it smoothed temperature swings and made powered operations more manageable. The catch was scheduling: the exact same enclosure that protected the rover from the cold became a problem if we charged too frequently.</p>
  <p>The next step is a more flight-like model: actual garage geometry, Firefly trajectory and thermal interfaces, followed by thermal-vacuum testing to see how well the predictions hold up.</p>
