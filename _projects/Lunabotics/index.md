## Introduction

Future lunar infrastructure will require machines that can move, excavate, and shape regolith without constant human control. NASA Lunabotics frames that challenge as an autonomous excavation and berm-construction problem. For CMU Moon Miners, the objective was to turn a first-year student team into a functioning rover program with a clear design process, a realistic test arena, defined subsystem ownership, and hardware that could survive integration rather than only looking complete in CAD.

## Methods

I served as project lead and helped connect mechanical, avionics, software, and systems work into one buildable rover. I translated competition rules and advisor feedback into requirements, interface documents, design reviews, and build priorities. The team used SRR, PDR, and CDR as hard gates so that chassis, drivetrain, excavation, sensing, controls, power, and serviceability decisions were reviewed before manufacturing. This process also forced the team to define interfaces early, including power cutoffs, sensor mast placement, harnessing, and mechanical access for testing.

## Results

The project produced a full rover architecture, a test arena at the Robotics Innovation Center, and a working team process for requirements, trade studies, risk tracking, manufacturing planning, and verification planning. Reviewer feedback led to practical design changes, including a simpler emergency-stop architecture, tighter power-domain separation, clearer sensor-field-of-view requirements, and path-planning requirements that accounted for heading and turning-radius limits.

## Discussion

The most valuable result was not a single mechanism. It was the creation of a repeatable engineering process for a new robotics team. The rover still had to be manufactured, tested, and improved, but the project moved from loose ambition to a system with traceable requirements, review-backed design decisions, and a test environment for validation. This project demonstrates systems engineering, mechanical design, cross-functional leadership, technical communication, and the ability to make design decisions around risk and integration.
